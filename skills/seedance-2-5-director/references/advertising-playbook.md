# Advertising & Commercial Playbook

Use this playbook for paid ads, product videos, ecommerce, branded social, luxury commercials, food ads, beauty campaigns, service ads and conversion-focused creative.

The objective is not merely to make footage look expensive. The video must communicate one commercial idea clearly.

---

## 1. Start with the ad job

Before writing the prompt, choose the main job:

- stop the scroll
- show the problem
- create desire
- demonstrate the product
- prove a result
- reveal before/after
- show craftsmanship
- establish trust
- make the product feel premium
- explain one feature
- land a memorable visual
- create a clean brand / CTA frame

One video should normally have one dominant job.

---

## 2. Commercial hierarchy

Plan in this order:

```text
AUDIENCE PROBLEM / DESIRE
↓
ONE PRODUCT OR SERVICE PROMISE
↓
VISUAL HOOK
↓
DEMONSTRATION / PROOF
↓
EMOTIONAL PAYOFF
↓
PRODUCT / BRAND HERO FRAME
```

Do not bury the product under cinematic decoration.

---

## 3. 10-second performance ad structure

```text
0–2s   HOOK
2–5s   PROBLEM / DESIRE / DISCOVERY
5–8s   BENEFIT / DEMONSTRATION / PAYOFF
8–10s  HERO FRAME / CLEAN EDITING SPACE
```

### Hook rules

A hook should be visually understandable with sound off.

Good hook types:

- impossible-but-readable visual transformation
- macro texture
- dramatic before/after contrast
- product entering frame unexpectedly
- human reaction
- satisfying physical action
- unusual perspective
- problem shown instantly
- product shown at its most desirable moment

Do not use random explosions or visual chaos simply to stop attention.

---

## 4. 15-second ad structure

```text
0–3s    hook / tension
3–6s    reveal product or solution
6–10s   demonstration / benefit
10–13s  proof / result / emotional payoff
13–15s  hero frame / brand space
```

Keep the central product visually readable for enough time that a viewer can identify it.

---

## 5. 30-second cinematic commercial

Use a stronger arc:

```text
SETUP
→ DESIRE / PROBLEM
→ PRODUCT DISCOVERY
→ EXPERIENCE / DEMONSTRATION
→ ESCALATION
→ PAYOFF
→ HERO FRAME
```

For longer ads, do not add events simply to fill time. Add visual progression.

---

## 6. Product authority

For a real product reference, define it as authoritative.

```text
@Image1 controls exact product geometry, proportions, packaging, cap, material, colour and logo placement.
```

Then lock it:

```text
Preserve exact product geometry across all shots. Do not redesign, stretch, melt, duplicate, relabel or recolour the product.
```

When critical label text must be perfect, prefer adding the final text in post-production rather than expecting generative video to reproduce fine typography reliably.

---

## 7. Product readability rule

A product shot fails commercially if the product is technically present but not readable.

At least one beat should provide:

- stable product silhouette
- correct proportions
- clean edge separation
- enough duration to identify it
- uncluttered background
- controlled highlights
- no hand covering key geometry

---

## 8. Hero frame strategy

Every commercial should deliberately land somewhere.

A strong hero frame defines:

```text
product position
camera distance
camera height
orientation
background simplicity
light direction
negative space for copy
motion state
```

Example:

```text
Final frame: product centered slightly below the vertical midpoint, three-quarter angle, stable 85mm-feel beauty composition, dark gradient background, narrow rim light on both edges, subtle reflection beneath, clean negative space in the upper third for post-production headline, motion fully settled.
```

---

## 9. Leave copy for post when appropriate

For ads, the agent should distinguish between:

### Video-generated visual
- product
- human
- environment
- movement
- lighting
- physical sign / packaging reference when already present

### Post-production overlay
- headline
- price
- CTA
- offer date
- URL
- legal copy
- detailed typography

If the user needs social ad copy, design negative space for it rather than forcing the model to invent readable text.

---

## 10. Ad camera recipes

### Luxury product

```text
macro detail → controlled reveal → human interaction → stable 85mm hero
```

Use slow motion only for a meaningful material event such as liquid, fabric, jewellery or impact.

### Food

```text
macro texture → preparation action → steam / cut / pull-apart → hero composition
```

Prioritize physical appetite cues:

- steam from actual hot food
- realistic sauce viscosity
- crisp surface texture
- visible meat structure
- fresh condensation where appropriate
- plausible hand interaction

Avoid impossible floating ingredients unless the concept intentionally calls for stylization.

### Beauty / skincare

```text
skin texture close-up → product application → tactile result → clean beauty hero
```

Preserve natural skin texture. Avoid plastic skin unless explicitly requested.

### Fashion

```text
silhouette hook → movement / fabric response → detail → editorial hero
```

Lock garment design when reference accuracy matters.

### Automotive

```text
shape reveal → motion → mechanical / material detail → parked or moving hero
```

Preserve vehicle body design and wheel geometry. Maintain road contact and plausible suspension behaviour.

### Technology

```text
problem / idle state → feature activation → physical response → product hero
```

Avoid meaningless floating HUD elements unless part of the concept.

### Home / interior service

```text
problem detail → craft / installation → transformation → finished-room hero
```

Lock architecture to prevent walls, windows, fireplaces, doors and furniture from morphing.

---

## 11. Service advertising

When there is no physical product, show the transformation or trust signal.

Useful structures:

### Problem → Process → Result

```text
SHOT 1: visibly damaged / unfinished state
SHOT 2: one believable craft action
SHOT 3: finished result
SHOT 4: calm hero view of completed space
```

### Human trust

```text
expert arrives → inspects → performs one clear action → client sees finished result
```

Do not make service ads look like generic stock footage. Show details specific to the service.

---

## 12. UGC-style advertising

UGC should feel simpler than a luxury commercial.

Use:

- phone-camera framing
- natural lighting
- believable human gestures
- slight but controlled handheld movement
- practical environment
- one clear product demonstration

Avoid making every UGC ad look like a studio commercial with cinematic fog and perfect dolly shots.

---

## 13. Luxury advertising

Luxury is restraint.

Prefer:

- slower movement
- minimal props
- precise reflections
- tactile macro detail
- dark or neutral negative space
- fewer cuts
- elegant sound accents
- controlled human gestures
- stable final frame

Avoid:

- too many particles
- excessive lens flare
- flashy camera spins
- over-saturated neon
- cheap-looking random text
- constant speed ramps

---

## 14. Social ad retention

For vertical paid social:

- subject/product readable in first frame
- first visual change happens quickly
- new perspective every 2–3 seconds when useful
- one dominant message
- payoff before viewer loses context
- product reappears or remains visible near the end

Do not design a beautiful first 7 seconds and reveal the product only in the final half-second.

## 16. Commercial quality gate

Before finalizing, ask silently:

- can I identify the product/service quickly?
- is the first frame understandable?
- is there one commercial message?
- is the demonstration believable?
- does the product remain accurate?
- are hands and interactions physically plausible?
- does every shot help sell the idea?
- is the final frame usable for an ad edit?
- is there room for post-production copy if needed?
- does the sound support rather than distract?

If not, simplify.

## 17. Example: premium food ad

```text
AD OBJECTIVE
Make a fresh burger look tactile, hot and premium in a 10-second vertical ad.

FORMAT
9:16, 10 seconds, realistic commercial food cinematography.

REFERENCE AUTHORITY
@Image1 controls exact burger build, bun shape, patty thickness and ingredient order.

CONTINUITY LOCKS
Preserve the same burger throughout. No ingredient duplication, no melting geometry, no floating food.

0–2s — HOOK
Extreme macro of the freshly grilled patty as a thin layer of juice catches the side light. Camera slides laterally a few centimetres. Natural grill sizzle.
End state: meat texture is clearly readable.

2–5s — BUILD DESIRE
Medium macro three-quarter angle. Top bun lifts naturally by a human hand, revealing the patty and ingredients. Steam rises subtly from the hot meat. Sauce moves with believable viscosity.
End state: burger structure is clearly visible and appetizing.

5–8s — PAYOFF
Clean knife cuts through the burger once. Camera stays stable enough to preserve geometry. Interior remains structured rather than collapsing.
End state: cross-section is clearly visible.

8–10s — HERO
Whole burger stands on a dark clean surface. Slow micro push-in, soft warm key light, crisp rim on bun and meat, background falls to subtle bokeh. Clean upper-third space for copy.
End state: motion settles on a campaign-ready hero frame.

AUDIO
Realistic grill sizzle, soft knife contact, subtle low commercial music bed.

CONSTRAINTS
No extra ingredients, no duplicate burger, no warped hands, no impossible cheese stretch, no random text, preserve exact burger design.
```
