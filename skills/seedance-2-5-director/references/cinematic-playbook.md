# Cinematic Playbook

Use this playbook when the user asks for cinematic, film-like, premium, dramatic, realistic, trailer-like, editorial, luxury, moody, atmospheric, polished or visually expensive video.

The skill must translate those adjectives into visible production choices.

---

## 1. Cinematic decision stack

Choose in this order:

1. story emotion
2. visual subject priority
3. composition
4. shot size
5. camera height and angle
6. one primary camera movement
7. lens feel
8. focus behaviour
9. lighting logic
10. depth layers
11. atmosphere
12. sound design
13. final frame

Do not start by choosing a lens or colour grade before the visual objective is clear.

---

## 2. Translate mood into observable direction

| User says | Translate into |
|---|---|
| cinematic | deliberate framing + motivated camera + controlled lighting + depth + sound |
| premium | clean geometry, slower controlled motion, restrained highlights, fewer distractions |
| dramatic | stronger contrast, directional light, purposeful scale or close-up, slower anticipation before payoff |
| energetic | faster subject movement, tracking, foreground parallax, shorter but meaningful beats |
| intimate | close framing, minimal camera movement, natural eye line, shallow depth, quiet sound bed |
| futuristic | modern materials, controlled practical lights, clean interfaces, physically plausible tech motion |
| luxury | tactile materials, precise reflections, soft controlled light, elegant pacing, stable hero frame |
| gritty | natural texture, imperfect environment, harder practical light, restrained handheld movement |

## 3. Shot-size library

### Extreme wide
Use for geography, scale, isolation or reveal.

```text
extreme wide establishing shot, subject small within the architecture, clear foreground-midground-background separation
```

### Wide
Use for full-body action and spatial logic.

```text
wide full-body composition, both feet visible, clear distance between subject and key objects
```

### Medium
Default for action, interaction and dialogue.

```text
medium shot, torso and hand action readable, environment still present
```

### Medium close-up
Use for emotional focus without losing all context.

```text
medium close-up, shoulders and face dominant, background softly separated
```

### Close-up
Use for emotion, reaction, reveal or important detail.

```text
tight close-up, eyes sharply prioritized, controlled shallow depth of field
```

### Macro
Use for product, food, texture, mechanisms and material detail.

```text
macro detail, narrow focus plane, physically accurate texture, controlled specular highlights
```

---

## 4. Lens-feel library

Use only when it changes the shot.

### 24–28mm feel
- environmental
- energetic
- strong parallax
- close movement feels fast
- useful for action, architecture, immersive POV

### 35mm feel
- natural cinematic context
- strong default for narrative work
- subject and environment both readable

### 50mm feel
- balanced human perspective
- useful for medium shots and grounded realism

### 85mm feel
- portrait compression
- premium isolation
- beauty, fashion, luxury, emotional close-ups

### Macro / 90–100mm macro feel
- product surfaces
- food texture
- condensation
- machinery
- jewellery
- tactile details

Avoid impossible lens stacks such as `24mm macro telephoto portrait lens`.

---

## 5. Camera movement library

Every movement needs a visual purpose.

### Reveal

**Slow dolly-in**
Use when attention should intensify.

```text
slow dolly-in toward the subject, constant speed, framing tightens gradually without sudden zoom
```

**Foreground slide**
Use for discovery.

```text
camera slides laterally from behind a foreground object, gradually revealing the subject through natural parallax
```

**Rack-focus reveal**
Use when the viewer should discover a second subject or product.

```text
focus begins on foreground detail, then racks cleanly to the subject as the camera remains nearly static
```

**Crane-down reveal**
Use for scale moving into intimacy.

```text
camera descends from a high establishing position into a controlled medium composition
```

### Energy

**Side tracking**

```text
camera tracks parallel to the subject at matching speed, stable horizon, background parallax emphasizes motion
```

**Backward tracking**

```text
camera moves backward at walking/running speed while maintaining consistent subject scale and centered framing
```

**Controlled handheld follow**

```text
subtle shoulder-mounted micro-motion, no violent shake, camera follows one clear subject path
```

**Whip transition**
Only when a cut is intentional.

```text
fast whip-pan to camera-right creates motion blur that hides one planned cut into the next shot moving in the same direction
```

### Premium product

**Macro slider**

```text
slow 5–10 cm lateral slider move across the product surface, reflections moving predictably across the material
```

**Micro orbit**

```text
slow partial orbit around a stationary product, product remains centered while highlights travel across the edges
```

**Top-down descent**

```text
camera begins overhead and lowers vertically toward the product without rotation
```

**Micro push-in**

```text
very slow forward camera movement on the final hero composition, almost imperceptible but maintaining visual life
```

## 6. Motivated camera rule

Before using a camera move, finish this sentence:

> The camera moves because the viewer needs to ______.

Examples:

- discover the product
- feel the character's speed
- see the scale of the environment
- move from problem to solution
- inspect a material detail
- land on the emotional reaction
- reveal the final transformation

If there is no reason, keep the camera stable.

## 7. Lighting families

### Natural cinematic daylight

```text
soft directional daylight from camera-left, gentle negative fill camera-right, realistic window falloff, neutral skin tones, no artificial glow
```

### Golden-hour lifestyle

```text
low warm sun from behind the subject, soft frontal bounce, long natural shadows, restrained lens flare only when the sun enters frame
```

### Moody interior

```text
motivated tungsten practicals in the background, soft key from one side, darker negative fill on the opposite cheek, subtle atmospheric depth
```

### Luxury studio

```text
large soft key above and camera-left, controlled black negative fill, narrow rim light tracing product edges, clean gradient background, precise reflections
```

### Beauty / skincare

```text
large frontal diffused beauty source, gentle side shaping, smooth highlight rolloff, natural skin texture preserved, bright but not flat
```

### Futuristic tech

```text
cool practical strips integrated into the environment, one neutral key on the subject, controlled reflective highlights, no random neon everywhere
```

### Night exterior

```text
motivated street and storefront practicals, cool ambient sky fill, localized warm pools of light, wet surfaces only if weather supports them
```

## 8. Depth and composition

Strong cinematic frames often use three readable planes:

```text
FOREGROUND
object, reflection, doorway, vegetation, architecture edge

MIDGROUND
main subject / product

BACKGROUND
environmental context, practical lights, secondary movement
```

Do not add foreground objects only to look cinematic. They should frame, hide/reveal, create scale or provide parallax.

---

## 9. Focus behaviour

Useful focus patterns:

- focus locked to face during movement
- rack focus from object to person
- product stays sharp while foreground falls soft
- focus follows a hand entering frame
- macro focus transitions from texture to logo area

Avoid unnecessary focus hunting.

---

## 10. Movement pacing

### Premium / luxury
Slower camera, fewer cuts, tactile detail, deliberate final frame.

### Action
Clear acceleration, directional movement, strong screen direction, no random camera teleportation.

### Social cinematic
3–5 visual beats in 8–15 seconds. Perspective can change around every 2–3 seconds when each change adds information.

### Emotional narrative
Hold longer on face and reaction. Do not cut away before the emotion lands.

---

## 11. Atmosphere discipline

Use atmosphere only when motivated:

- rain because scene is wet
- steam because food is hot
- dust because environment contains loose particles
- haze because light shafts need depth
- smoke because source exists

Avoid universal AI-video tropes such as random sparks, floating dust and heavy fog in every scene.

---

## 12. Sound as cinematography

Sound changes perceived scale and realism.

### Examples

**Luxury product**
```text
quiet room tone, delicate glass contact, restrained sub-bass pulse, one clean final sonic accent
```

**Action**
```text
footsteps with surface weight, clothing movement, impacts aligned to contact, environmental reflections, music secondary to physical sound
```

**Food**
```text
oil sizzle, crisp surface crackle, knife contact, subtle steam hiss, no exaggerated cartoon sounds
```

**Tech**
```text
low mechanical servo movement, restrained digital confirmation tones, room ambience preserved
```

---

## 13. Cinematic transition library

Use generated transitions only when motivated.

### Match cut
Match shape, direction or composition between two shots.

### Occlusion cut
A foreground object fills the frame, hiding the edit.

### Whip cut
Motion blur in same direction bridges shots.

### Light wipe
A motivated bright source crosses frame and reveals the next scene.

### Hard cut
Often the cleanest choice. Do not hide every edit.

Avoid default cross dissolves between unrelated shots.

## 15. Cinematic anti-slop checklist

Remove or rewrite:

- `epic cinematic masterpiece`
- `insane dynamic camera`
- `dramatic lighting` without source/direction
- random fog
- random sparks
- meaningless anamorphic flare
- camera doing 4 movements at once
- impossible lens descriptions
- arbitrary speed ramps
- a cut every second
- endless orbit shots
- excessive slow motion
- unreadable action hidden by effects

A cleaner prompt often produces a more expensive-looking result.
